An **answer market** pays people and AI agents for information that changes an LLM judge's conclusion and survives criticism. The aim is to make knowledge held by someone else available when it would change your decision.  

An assistant can search published material, but useful information also lives in people's experience, unpublished work, and specialised tools. A former employee knows how a team operates. A researcher knows why a promising experiment failed. A supplier knows about an alternative you have never considered. Better search alone does not give those people a reason to contribute.  

## What contributors get paid for  

A sponsor states a question, the goal behind it, and a reward budget. The judge records its current answer and reasons. Contributors submit information, criticisms, or new options, explaining what should change. The reward depends on the contribution's effect under an explicit rubric for the sponsor's goal.  

Consider deciding whether to accept a startup job. Public information and interviews support accepting. A former employee supplies evidence that the advertised role lacks authority over the work you care about. That changes the recommendation. Someone else identifies an open role that better meets your criteria, expanding the choice rather than merely arguing against the original offer.  

Both contributions must survive scrutiny. A response might show that the former employee's information predates a reorganisation. Another might show that the alternative role requires a move you cannot make. Criticisms can themselves be criticised. The judge evaluates these relationships, and rewards depend on what remains relevant at the end of the review period, rather than on each temporary reversal.  

Paying on final status avoids rewarding an initial claim and its later correction as two successive improvements. It does not by itself settle how to divide credit among overlapping contributions. That requires a stated attribution rule.  

## Why use this instead of a prediction market?  

A prediction-market trader earns money by buying an outcome below its eventual payout, or selling above it. Knowing something useful is insufficient: the trader must also judge whether the price already reflects it. A contributor can be right about the underlying issue and still lose money on the position.  

An answer market evaluates the submitted contribution directly. The contributor need not buy an outcome position to discover whether their information improves the answer. Research effort still costs time, and a submission stake or reputation requirement can deter spam. The claim is narrower than risk-free participation: usefulness is assessed against the judge's recorded reasoning, rather than expressed through a speculative position.  

[Opportunity markets](https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/opportunity-markets) also pay people who identify opportunities, using sponsor-funded markets with temporarily private prices. Their traders can have an incentive to explain their reasoning to the sponsor. An answer market makes the contribution and its surviving effect the object of payment.  

The sponsor receives an inspectable reason to reconsider. They can evaluate one useful correction before delegating an entire decision procedure.  

## The engineering question  

The judge must recognise valid criticism and reject misleading objections. Recursive review creates opportunities to correct mistakes, but a judge that repeatedly accepts the same fallacy can still fail. [Testing answer-market judges](https://distbit.xyz/testing-answer-market-judges/) describes a comparison that measures both accepted wrong answers and rejected correct ones.  

This is a mechanism proposal. [Truesight](https://distbit.xyz/truesight-recursive-criticism/) explores its recursive criticism component; it is not a deployed, validated reward market. Earlier [dialog markets](https://stuhlmueller.org/dialog-markets/) examine rewards for useful contributions to a conversation, while [Information Bazaar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14443) studies language agents inspecting and buying information. The opportunity here is to connect that information to a particular decision and pay for improvements that withstand challenge.
